How it works

From a CV in your files to a pack in the queue.

Six stages, each one doing one job. The scoring is rules, the rewriting is grounded, and the last decision stays with you.

The pipeline

What happens, in order

The server and the database sit under your control. The outbound calls are the ones the feature needs: the public job boards, a model provider only when one is configured, and the payment provider at checkout.

Ingest

Paste the CV as text, or upload .txt, .md, .docx, .pdf, .odt, .rtf or .html, or an image read with OCR. The document is converted to plain text and segmented into a contact block, summary, experience, education and skills. A CV with no headings at all still works: the lines that match nothing else land in an other section rather than being dropped.

The database holds the text, never the uploaded file. A document that yields no text is refused with the reason; anything only partly readable comes back with warnings. A CV shorter than 80 characters is refused as too thin to analyse.

Score

Paste the job description, or attach one from the feed. Five components are scored out of 100 and then weighted into a single number, and every point you did not get arrives as a finding with a severity, a detail and a suggested fix.

The scorer is deterministic: no network, no model, no clock. The same CV and the same job text always produce the same report, which is why two runs can be compared.

Tailor

The summary and the bullets are rewritten for that job using only the facts in the CV and the structured profile. Rephrasing and re-ordering are allowed. A new employer, title, qualification, date or number is not.

There are two paths: one model call that returns structured JSON and is validated against a schema, or a deterministic rule-based rewriter when no model is configured. Both run the same grounding check, and a bullet that fails it is reset to your own wording with a warning that says so.

Package

The pack is a print-ready CV, a covering letter and answers to the common screening questions: motivation, notice period, right to work and salary expectation. Where the profile does not state an answer, the pack says it is a placeholder instead of inventing one.

The CV document is A4 with 18mm margins, a single column, system fonts, real text headings, no images, no icon fonts and no tables used for layout. It survives an ATS parse and a paste into Word.

Track

Each application carries a status, the score it was built against, the pack and a timeline. The statuses run pack ready, approved, submitted, interview, rejected and offer, with withdrawn available when a role stops being worth pursuing.

Approved and ready is where the product stops. You open the employer's own page, apply, then move the record to submitted. No form is filled in for you.

Alert

A saved search holds keywords, locations, a remote-only flag and a minimum salary. When it runs, it queries the public boards, matches each posting against the rule, and drops new matches into the notifications inbox and the account's RSS feed.

The cadence, daily, weekly or manual, and the last run time are stored with the alert, so you can see how stale a search is before you rely on it.

The scorer

What the score is made of

Five components, fixed weights, no hidden adjustment. The report and any screen that shows a score read the same numbers.

Each component is scored out of 100 and then weighted, so the five weights add up to the total score out of 100.
Component Weight What it counts
Keywords 35 The share of the job's key terms that appear in your CV, weighted by how often each term occurs and how rare it is across the two documents.
Structure 20 A readable contact block, an experience section, dates on the roles, a summary, and education or skills.
Achievements 20 Bullets that carry a number, bullets that open with a strong verb, and evidence that the work produced an outcome.
Readability 15 Total length, how many sentences and bullets run past 30 words, and how many run past 45.
Formatting 10 Deductions for missing headings or contact details, overlong paragraphs and bullets, blocks of capitals, inconsistent dates, and layout tables.

Findings are specific: which two of fourteen job keywords appear in the experience section, which dates disagree, which bullet has no figure in it. Generic advice is not produced.

Grounding

What grounding means

A tailored bullet is a rewrite of a line you already wrote, not a new claim about your career.

Grounding is a check that runs on every rewritten bullet, on both the model path and the rule-based path. Every number in the rewrite must already appear in the source line. No new employer, job title, qualification or date may be introduced. A rewrite that breaks either rule is rejected and the original wording is restored.

The rewrite may still do useful work: it can move the number to the front, cut the filler, use the employer's own vocabulary for the same fact, and put the bullet in the position the job description cares about. It cannot upgrade supported to led unless the source says so.

Model output is also checked for schema shape and for naming the company and role in the covering letter. Anything that fails is replaced with the rule-based version, and the pack lists the replacement under warnings rather than hiding it.

Limits

Where the edges are

A tool that hides its limits makes you find them at the worst moment. These are the ones worth knowing before you start.

No scraping, no submission

LinkedIn is not read and no third-party form is filled in. Capture is user-initiated: paste the URL or the page text, and the parser names what it could not find instead of guessing.

PDFs, scans and images

PDF text is extracted with a local pdftotext binary (native, or through WSL). Scanned PDFs are not OCRed — export the pages as images first. Without a binary a PDF upload is refused with the reason shown. .txt, .md and .docx are read in-process with no external tool.

Rules, not judgement

The scorer counts headings, numbers and keywords. It cannot know whether a bullet is true, only whether it is quantified, structured and covered by the job's terms.

Salary comparisons are literal

A minimum-salary rule matches only when the posting states an annual figure. Hourly, daily and weekly rates are recorded but not annualised, because doing that would need assumptions about the working year.

Alerts are triggered

Saving an alert records a cadence, a rule and a last-run time. Checks run when they are triggered from the app. A scheduled scan on our server re-runs your saved searches while you are away; nothing is polled from your browser, and you can delete an alert at any time.

The pack is deliberately plain

A4, 18mm margins, one column, system fonts, no images and no layout tables. The format is chosen to survive an ATS parse and a paste into Word, not to win a design award.
