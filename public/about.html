Why it exists

One CV, many job descriptions

A single CV has to answer many job descriptions at once. The part that makes an application worth reading, tailoring the summary and the bullets to the role in front of you, is also the part that gets skipped when a search is running at volume.

That is not laziness. It is the cost of doing a careful job by hand, application after application, while also chasing replies and keeping a spreadsheet of what went where.

Applywright is built to take the repetitive half of that work and leave the judgement half alone. It scores a CV against a job, explains the score in findings you can act on, and produces a tailored pack from facts that are already in the CV. It then keeps the record: which pack went to which employer, what happened next, and which saved search found it.

It is an application workspace for someone who is serious about a search, not a spraying machine. The difference matters, and it decides most of the design.

How this is built

Position

Why not an auto-apply bot

An auto-apply service optimises for the number of applications sent. The number is the wrong measure.

Submitting to a third-party site without a person present breaches that site's terms, and the account it risks is the one the search depends on. It also produces applications the candidate cannot discuss in an interview, for roles they may not have read, with claims nobody can defend. Volume of that kind is not a shortcut; it is a debt collected later.

We do not think a CV worth sending can be produced that way, so we do not build it. The product prepares, checks and records. The person sends.

What that rules out

Scraping LinkedIn or any site that forbids it. Filling in an employer's application form for you. Inventing an employer, a title, a date or a number to fit a job. Sending anything before you have read it.

What that leaves

A grounded rewrite you can stand behind, a score you can argue with, a queue of applications that are approved and ready, and a record that tells you what happened to each one.

Principles

Four rules the product is built on

Each one costs the product a feature it could have sold. That is the point.

Principle 01

Grounded or nothing

Every rewrite is traceable to a line in the source CV. A grounding check compares each rewritten bullet with its original and rejects any new number, employer, title, qualification or date. A bullet that fails is reset to your own wording, and the pack lists the reset under warnings.

Principle 02

Named findings, not a verdict

A score is only useful if it tells you what to change. Every lost point carries a finding with a severity, a detail and a fix, naming the missing keywords, the inconsistent dates or the bullet with no figure in it.

Principle 03

The decision stays with you

The queue ends at approved and ready. You open the employer's page, apply, and record what happened. Capture of a posting is something you start, not something that happens in the background.

Principle 04

Delete means delete

The database holds the text of your CV, never the uploaded file. Export returns everything you own as JSON, and deleting the account removes your CVs, reports, packs, alerts and application records with it.
