The flow

Ingest, score, tailor. Then send it yourself.

Three steps do the work. The fourth is yours, and the product keeps it that way.

Step 01

Ingest

Paste your CV or upload .txt, .md or .docx. Applywright segments it into contact details, summary, experience, education and skills, and stores the text rather than the original file.

PDF is read when pdftotext is installed on the machine; otherwise the upload is refused with a warning that says why.

Step 02

Score

Paste the job description. Five weighted components produce a score out of 100, and every lost point arrives as a named finding with the detail and a fix, such as which job keywords are missing from your experience section.

No model is involved. The same CV and the same job text always produce the same report.

Step 03

Tailor

The summary and the bullets are rewritten for that job using only the facts in your CV and the profile you fill in. A rewritten bullet that introduces a number, employer, title or date fails the grounding check and falls back to your own wording.

Output: a print-ready, single-column CV, a covering letter and answers to the common screening questions.

What it refuses to do

Grounded rewriting, named findings, no auto-apply.

The three things that make a tailored CV worth sending are the three things an application robot cannot give you.

Grounded rewriting

Your facts, reworded

Tailoring may rephrase and re-order. It may not add an employer, a title, a qualification, a date or a number. Every rewritten bullet is compared with its source line, and one that fails the check is reset to the wording you already had.

Named findings

A score with reasons

Every point is explained by a finding with a severity, a detail and a fix. Nothing says improve your CV; it says which keywords are missing, which dates are inconsistent and which bullets carry no number.

No auto-apply

You send it

The queue ends at approved and ready. You click apply on the employer's own page, then record what happened. Nothing is submitted to a third-party site on your behalf, and nothing is captured from a site you did not paste in yourself.

The same reason rules out scraping LinkedIn. It breaches the site's terms, it gets the account you apply with restricted, and it produces applications you would have to defend in an interview.

Questions

Straight answers

Do you apply for me?

No. Applywright prepares the pack and stops at approved and ready; you click apply on the employer's own page and record what happened. Automated submission to third-party sites breaches their terms, puts the account your search depends on at risk, and produces applications you would have to answer for in an interview. The queue is deliberately a queue.

Do you scrape LinkedIn?

No. The job feed uses three public boards, Arbeitnow, Remotive and Remote OK, through their own APIs. Capturing a posting is something you start: you paste the URL, or the page text when the page renders in JavaScript. Nothing is crawled in the background.

Can it invent experience to make me fit?

No. Rewriting is limited to the facts in your CV and the profile you fill in. A grounding check rejects any number, employer, title, qualification or date that is not already in the source, and a bullet that fails is reset to your own wording. If the model path is unavailable, a rule-based rewriter runs instead and says so in its warnings.

What does the score actually measure?

Five weighted parts: keyword coverage 35, structure 20, quantified achievements 20, readability 15 and formatting risks 10. The scorer is deterministic rules, not a model, so the same CV against the same job description always produces the same number and the same findings.

What happens to my CV?

The text is stored, never the original file. You can export everything you own as JSON, or delete the account from Settings, which removes your CVs, reports, packs, alerts and application records from the database.
